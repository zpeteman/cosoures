% ----------- EXERCISE 1 -------------
\section{}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item State Koenig's theorem relating to the angular momentum of a perfect rigid body $S$, in motion relative to a reference frame $T_1$.
    \item Prove this theorem.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{correctionbox}
\textbf{1. Statement of Koenig's theorem:}

For a rigid body $S$ with center of mass $G$, the angular momentum about any point $O$ is given by:
$$\vec{\sigma}_O(S/T_1) = \vec{\sigma}_G(S/T_1) + m\vec{OG} \times \vec{V}_G(S/T_1)$$

Where:
- $\vec{\sigma}_O(S/T_1)$ is the angular momentum of $S$ about point $O$ relative to frame $T_1$
- $\vec{\sigma}_G(S/T_1)$ is the angular momentum of $S$ about its center of mass $G$ relative to frame $T_1$
- $m$ is the total mass of the rigid body
- $\vec{V}_G(S/T_1)$ is the velocity of the center of mass relative to frame $T_1$

\textbf{2. Proof:}

Let $P$ be an arbitrary point of the rigid body $S$ with mass $dm$. The angular momentum about $O$ is:
$$\vec{\sigma}_O(S/T_1) = \int_{P \in S} \vec{OP} \times \vec{V}_P(S/T_1)  dm$$

We can write $\vec{OP} = \vec{OG} + \vec{GP}$, so:
$$\vec{\sigma}_O(S/T_1) = \int_{P \in S} (\vec{OG} + \vec{GP}) \times \vec{V}_P(S/T_1)  dm$$
$$= \vec{OG} \times \int_{P \in S} \vec{V}_P(S/T_1)  dm + \int_{P \in S} \vec{GP} \times \vec{V}_P(S/T_1)  dm$$

For a rigid body, we have the velocity distribution formula:
$$\vec{V}_P(S/T_1) = \vec{V}_G(S/T_1) + \vec{\omega}_{S/T_1} \times \vec{GP}$$

Substituting this into the first integral:
$$\int_{P \in S} \vec{V}_P(S/T_1)  dm = \int_{P \in S} [\vec{V}_G(S/T_1) + \vec{\omega}_{S/T_1} \times \vec{GP}]  dm$$
$$= m\vec{V}_G(S/T_1) + \vec{\omega}_{S/T_1} \times \int_{P \in S} \vec{GP}  dm$$

But $\int_{P \in S} \vec{GP}  dm = 0$ by definition of the center of mass, so:
$$\int_{P \in S} \vec{V}_P(S/T_1)  dm = m\vec{V}_G(S/T_1)$$

Now for the second integral:
$$\int_{P \in S} \vec{GP} \times \vec{V}_P(S/T_1)  dm = \int_{P \in S} \vec{GP} \times [\vec{V}_G(S/T_1) + \vec{\omega}_{S/T_1} \times \vec{GP}]  dm$$
$$= \int_{P \in S} \vec{GP} \times \vec{V}_G(S/T_1)  dm + \int_{P \in S} \vec{GP} \times (\vec{\omega}_{S/T_1} \times \vec{GP})  dm$$

The first term is:
$$\int_{P \in S} \vec{GP} \times \vec{V}_G(S/T_1)  dm = \left(\int_{P \in S} \vec{GP}  dm\right) \times \vec{V}_G(S/T_1) = 0$$

The second term is exactly the definition of the angular momentum about $G$:
$$\int_{P \in S} \vec{GP} \times (\vec{\omega}_{S/T_1} \times \vec{GP})  dm = \vec{\sigma}_G(S/T_1)$$

Therefore:
$$\vec{\sigma}_O(S/T_1) = \vec{OG} \times m\vec{V}_G(S/T_1) + \vec{\sigma}_G(S/T_1)$$

This completes the proof of Koenig's theorem.
\end{correctionbox}

% ----------- EXERCISE 2 -------------
\section{}

A system $S$ consists of a homogeneous rod $S_1$ of mass $m_1$ and length $l$, in motion, in the plane $O_1X_1Y_1$, about the axis $O_1Z$ of the frame $T_1(O_1, X_1, Y_1, Z_1)$, and a disk $S_2$, of mass $m_2$, radius $R$, and center $C$, in motion about the axis $CZ_1$ (fig 1). We denote by $\theta$ the angle that indicates the position of the rod with respect to $T_1$ and by $\varphi$ the angle that indicates the position of the disk with respect to $T_1$. $(\vec{i}, \vec{j}, \vec{k}_1)$ is the basis of the frame $T(O, X, Y, Z_1)$ attached to the rod.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Determine the position of the center of mass $G$ of the system.
    \item Calculate the kinetic elements of system $S$ with respect to $T_1$, at point $O_1$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{correctionbox}
\textbf{1. Position of the center of mass $G$:}

Let's place the coordinate system such that $O_1$ is at the origin. The rod extends from $O_1$ to a point at distance $l$ along the rod's axis.

For the homogeneous rod $S_1$, its center of mass is at its midpoint:
$$G_1: \vec{O_1G_1} = \frac{l}{2}\vec{i}$$

For the disk $S_2$, its center $C$ is at the end of the rod:
$$\vec{O_1C} = l\vec{i}$$

The center of mass $G$ of the entire system is given by:
$$\vec{O_1G} = \frac{m_1\vec{O_1G_1} + m_2\vec{O_1C}}{m_1 + m_2} = \frac{m_1 \cdot \frac{l}{2}\vec{i} + m_2 \cdot l\vec{i}}{m_1 + m_2} = \frac{l(m_1/2 + m_2)}{m_1 + m_2}\vec{i}$$

\textbf{2. Kinetic elements at point $O_1$:}

The kinetic torsor has two elements: the momentum (resultant) and the angular momentum (moment).

\textbf{a) Momentum:}
$$\vec{P}(S/T_1) = (m_1 + m_2)\vec{V}_G(S/T_1)$$

We need $\vec{V}_G(S/T_1)$. From the position:
$$\vec{O_1G} = \frac{l(m_1/2 + m_2)}{m_1 + m_2}\vec{i}$$

Differentiating with respect to time in frame $T_1$:
$$\vec{V}_G(S/T_1) = \frac{d}{dt}(\vec{O_1G})_{T_1} = \frac{l(m_1/2 + m_2)}{m_1 + m_2} \frac{d\vec{i}}{dt}$$

Since $\vec{i}$ is fixed in the rod frame which rotates with angular velocity $\dot{\theta}\vec{k}_1$ relative to $T_1$:
$$\frac{d\vec{i}}{dt} = \vec{\omega}_{T/T_1} \times \vec{i} = \dot{\theta}\vec{k}_1 \times \vec{i} = \dot{\theta}\vec{j}$$

Therefore:
$$\vec{V}_G(S/T_1) = \frac{l(m_1/2 + m_2)}{m_1 + m_2} \dot{\theta}\vec{j}$$

And the momentum is:
$$\vec{P}(S/T_1) = (m_1 + m_2)\vec{V}_G(S/T_1) = l(m_1/2 + m_2)\dot{\theta}\vec{j}$$

\textbf{b) Angular momentum at $O_1$:}

We use the additive property for composite systems:
$$\vec{\sigma}_{O_1}(S/T_1) = \vec{\sigma}_{O_1}(S_1/T_1) + \vec{\sigma}_{O_1}(S_2/T_1)$$

For the rod $S_1$ rotating about $O_1Z$:
$$\vec{\sigma}_{O_1}(S_1/T_1) = I_{O_1Z}(S_1)\dot{\theta}\vec{k}_1$$

Where $I_{O_1Z}(S_1)$ is the moment of inertia of the rod about axis $O_1Z$. For a thin rod of length $l$ and mass $m_1$ rotating about one end:
$$I_{O_1Z}(S_1) = \frac{1}{3}m_1l^2$$

So:
$$\vec{\sigma}_{O_1}(S_1/T_1) = \frac{1}{3}m_1l^2\dot{\theta}\vec{k}_1$$

For the disk $S_2$, we use Koenig's theorem:
$$\vec{\sigma}_{O_1}(S_2/T_1) = \vec{\sigma}_C(S_2/T_1) + m_2\vec{O_1C} \times \vec{V}_C(S_2/T_1)$$

The disk rotates about $CZ_1$ with angular velocity $\dot{\varphi}\vec{k}_1$, so:
$$\vec{\sigma}_C(S_2/T_1) = I_{CZ}(S_2)\dot{\varphi}\vec{k}_1$$

For a disk about its central axis perpendicular to its plane:
$$I_{CZ}(S_2) = \frac{1}{2}m_2R^2$$

Now, $\vec{O_1C} = l\vec{i}$ and $\vec{V}_C(S_2/T_1) = \frac{d}{dt}(l\vec{i})_{T_1} = l\dot{\theta}\vec{j}$

So:
$$m_2\vec{O_1C} \times \vec{V}_C(S_2/T_1) = m_2(l\vec{i}) \times (l\dot{\theta}\vec{j}) = m_2l^2\dot{\theta}(\vec{i} \times \vec{j}) = m_2l^2\dot{\theta}\vec{k}_1$$

Therefore:
$$\vec{\sigma}_{O_1}(S_2/T_1) = \frac{1}{2}m_2R^2\dot{\varphi}\vec{k}_1 + m_2l^2\dot{\theta}\vec{k}_1$$

The total angular momentum is:
$$\vec{\sigma}_{O_1}(S/T_1) = \left(\frac{1}{3}m_1l^2\dot{\theta} + m_2l^2\dot{\theta} + \frac{1}{2}m_2R^2\dot{\varphi}\right)\vec{k}_1$$
$$= \left(l^2\left(\frac{m_1}{3} + m_2\right)\dot{\theta} + \frac{1}{2}m_2R^2\dot{\varphi}\right)\vec{k}_1$$

The kinetic torsor at $O_1$ is therefore:
$$\tau(S/T_1) = \left[l(m_1/2 + m_2)\dot{\theta}\vec{j}, \left(l^2\left(\frac{m_1}{3} + m_2\right)\dot{\theta} + \frac{1}{2}m_2R^2\dot{\varphi}\right)\vec{k}_1\right]$$
\end{correctionbox}

% ----------- EXERCISE 3 -------------
\section{}

A homogeneous rod of length $l$ and mass $m$, rotates, in the vertical plane $XOY$, about the axis $OZ$ of a reference frame $R(O, X, Y, Z)$ assumed to be Galilean. The position of the rod with respect to $R$ is indicated by the angle $\theta$ (fig 2). We admit that the torsor of contact actions at $O$ exerted on the rod reduces to the reaction $\vec{R}$.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item By applying the angular momentum theorem to the rod, at $O$, find a first integral of motion, knowing that $\dot{\theta} = \omega_0$ when $\theta = 0$.
    \item By applying the momentum theorem, determine the expression of the reaction.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{correctionbox}
\textbf{1. First integral of motion using angular momentum theorem:}

The angular momentum theorem at point $O$ states:
$$\frac{d\vec{\sigma}_O}{dt} = \vec{M}_O(\text{external forces})$$

The external forces are:
- Weight: $mg$ acting at the center of mass $G$
- Reaction at $O$: $\vec{R}$

The moment of the reaction at $O$ about $O$ is zero. The moment of the weight about $O$ is:
$$\vec{M}_O(\text{weight}) = \vec{OG} \times m\vec{g}$$

The center of mass $G$ is at distance $l/2$ from $O$ along the rod. In the rotating frame attached to the rod:
$$\vec{OG} = \frac{l}{2}\vec{i}$$

Where $\vec{i}$ is the unit vector along the rod. The gravitational acceleration is:
$$\vec{g} = -g\vec{j}$$

The angular momentum of the rod about $O$ is:
$$\vec{\sigma}_O = I_O\dot{\theta}\vec{k}$$

Where $I_O$ is the moment of inertia of the rod about $O$. For a thin rod of length $l$ and mass $m$ rotating about one end:
$$I_O = \frac{1}{3}ml^2$$

So:
$$\vec{\sigma}_O = \frac{1}{3}ml^2\dot{\theta}\vec{k}$$

Now, the angular momentum theorem gives:
$$\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{3}ml^2\dot{\theta}\vec{k}\right) = \vec{OG} \times m\vec{g}$$

The left side is:
$$\frac{1}{3}ml^2\ddot{\theta}\vec{k}$$

The right side is:
$$\vec{OG} \times m\vec{g} = \left(\frac{l}{2}\vec{i}\right) \times (-mg\vec{j}) = -\frac{mgl}{2}(\vec{i} \times \vec{j}) = -\frac{mgl}{2}\vec{k}$$

So we have:
$$\frac{1}{3}ml^2\ddot{\theta} = -\frac{mgl}{2}$$

Simplifying:
$$\ddot{\theta} = -\frac{3g}{2l}$$

This is a differential equation we can integrate. Multiply both sides by $2\dot{\theta}$:
$$2\dot{\theta}\ddot{\theta} = -\frac{3g}{l}\dot{\theta}$$

Integrate with respect to time:
$$\dot{\theta}^2 = -\frac{3g}{l}\theta + C$$

Using the initial condition $\dot{\theta} = \omega_0$ when $\theta = 0$:
$$\omega_0^2 = C$$

So the first integral is:
$$\dot{\theta}^2 = \omega_0^2 - \frac{3g}{l}\theta$$

\textbf{2. Reaction using momentum theorem:}

The momentum theorem states:
$$m\vec{a}_G = \vec{R} + m\vec{g}$$

We need the acceleration of the center of mass $G$. The position of $G$ is:
$$\vec{OG} = \frac{l}{2}\vec{i}$$

Differentiating twice with respect to time:
$$\vec{V}_G = \frac{l}{2}\dot{\theta}\vec{j}$$
$$\vec{a}_G = \frac{l}{2}\ddot{\theta}\vec{j} - \frac{l}{2}\dot{\theta}^2\vec{i}$$

We already have $\ddot{\theta} = -\frac{3g}{2l}$ and $\dot{\theta}^2 = \omega_0^2 - \frac{3g}{l}\theta$

So:
$$\vec{a}_G = \frac{l}{2}\left(-\frac{3g}{2l}\right)\vec{j} - \frac{l}{2}\left(\omega_0^2 - \frac{3g}{l}\theta\right)\vec{i}$$
$$= -\frac{3g}{4}\vec{j} - \frac{l}{2}\left(\omega_0^2 - \frac{3g}{l}\theta\right)\vec{i}$$

Now from the momentum theorem:
$$\vec{R} = m\vec{a}_G - m\vec{g} = m\vec{a}_G + mg\vec{j}$$

Substituting $\vec{a}_G$:
$$\vec{R} = m\left[-\frac{l}{2}\left(\omega_0^2 - \frac{3g}{l}\theta\right)\vec{i} - \frac{3g}{4}\vec{j}\right] + mg\vec{j}$$
$$= -\frac{ml}{2}\left(\omega_0^2 - \frac{3g}{l}\theta\right)\vec{i} + \left(mg - \frac{3mg}{4}\right)\vec{j}$$
$$= -\frac{ml}{2}\left(\omega_0^2 - \frac{3g}{l}\theta\right)\vec{i} + \frac{mg}{4}\vec{j}$$

So the reaction at $O$ is:
$$\vec{R} = -\frac{ml}{2}\left(\omega_0^2 - \frac{3g}{l}\theta\right)\vec{i} + \frac{mg}{4}\vec{j}$$
\end{correctionbox}

% ----------- EXERCISE 4 -------------
\section{}

A homogeneous bar $AB$, of length $2l$ and mass $m$, rests on a point $E$ located at a height $h$ above the horizontal plane as shown in figure 3. Under the action of the force $\vec{F}_A$, point $A$ moves on the axis $OX$ with a constant velocity $v_A$. The contacts at points $A$ and $E$ are frictionless. The position of the bar, with respect to the terrestrial reference frame $R(O, X, Y, Z)$ assumed to be Galilean, is defined by the angle $\theta$. The distance $OH$ is constant.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Express $\dot{\theta}$ and $\ddot{\theta}$ as a function of $\theta$ and $v_A$.
    \item Determine, in the basis of $R$ and as a function of $\dot{\theta}$, the momentum of bar $AB$ and its angular momentum at point $A$.
    \item By applying the angular momentum theorem to bar $AB$ at point $A$, determine the expression of the reaction $\vec{R}_E$ exerted on the bar at point $E$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{correctionbox}
\textbf{1. Expressing $\dot{\theta}$ and $\ddot{\theta}$:}

From the geometry, point $A$ has coordinates $(x_A, 0)$ and point $E$ has coordinates $(0, h)$. The bar has length $2l$.

The distance between $A$ and $E$ is:
$$AE = \sqrt{x_A^2 + h^2}$$

But also from the bar's geometry, the vertical projection gives:
$$h = 2l\sin\theta$$

And the horizontal distance from $A$ to the vertical through $E$ is:
$$x_A = 2l\cos\theta - OH$$

Since $OH$ is constant and $v_A = \dot{x}_A$ is constant, we have:
$$x_A = 2l\cos\theta - OH$$
$$\dot{x}_A = -2l\dot{\theta}\sin\theta = v_A$$

Therefore:
$$\dot{\theta} = -\frac{v_A}{2l\sin\theta}$$

Differentiating again:
$$\ddot{\theta} = \frac{v_A}{2l}\cdot\frac{\dot{\theta}\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta} = \frac{v_A}{2l}\cdot\left(-\frac{v_A}{2l\sin\theta}\right)\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta} = -\frac{v_A^2\cos\theta}{4l^2\sin^3\theta}$$

\textbf{2. Momentum and angular momentum at point $A$:}

The center of mass $G$ is at the midpoint of the bar. Its position relative to $A$ is:
$$\vec{AG} = l(\cos\theta\vec{i} + \sin\theta\vec{j})$$

The velocity of $G$ is:
$$\vec{V}_G = \vec{V}_A + \vec{\omega} \times \vec{AG}$$

Since $\vec{V}_A = v_A\vec{i}$ and $\vec{\omega} = \dot{\theta}\vec{k}$:
$$\vec{V}_G = v_A\vec{i} + \dot{\theta}\vec{k} \times l(\cos\theta\vec{i} + \sin\theta\vec{j})$$
$$= v_A\vec{i} + l\dot{\theta}(-\sin\theta\vec{i} + \cos\theta\vec{j})$$
$$= (v_A - l\dot{\theta}\sin\theta)\vec{i} + l\dot{\theta}\cos\theta\vec{j}$$

The momentum is:
$$\vec{P} = m\vec{V}_G = m(v_A - l\dot{\theta}\sin\theta)\vec{i} + ml\dot{\theta}\cos\theta\vec{j}$$

The angular momentum at point $A$ is:
$$\vec{\sigma}_A = I_A\dot{\theta}\vec{k}$$

Where $I_A$ is the moment of inertia of the bar about point $A$. For a thin bar of length $2l$ and mass $m$ about one end:
$$I_A = \frac{1}{3}m(2l)^2 = \frac{4}{3}ml^2$$

So:
$$\vec{\sigma}_A = \frac{4}{3}ml^2\dot{\theta}\vec{k}$$

\textbf{3. Reaction $\vec{R}_E$ using angular momentum theorem at $A$:}

The angular momentum theorem at point $A$ states:
$$\frac{d\vec{\sigma}_A}{dt} = \vec{M}_A(\text{external forces})$$

The external forces are:
- Weight: $mg$ acting at $G$
- Reaction at $E$: $\vec{R}_E = R_{Ex}\vec{i} + R_{Ey}\vec{j}$
- Force at $A$: $\vec{F}_A$ (but its moment about $A$ is zero)

The moment of the weight about $A$ is:
$$\vec{M}_A(\text{weight}) = \vec{AG} \times m\vec{g} = l(\cos\theta\vec{i} + \sin\theta\vec{j}) \times (-mg\vec{j}) = -mgl\cos\theta(\vec{i} \times \vec{j}) = -mgl\cos\theta\vec{k}$$

The moment of the reaction at $E$ about $A$ is:
$$\vec{M}_A(\vec{R}_E) = \vec{AE} \times \vec{R}_E$$

We have $\vec{AE} = -x_A\vec{i} + h\vec{j} = (-2l\cos\theta + OH)\vec{i} + 2l\sin\theta\vec{j}$

So:
$$\vec{AE} \times \vec{R}_E = 
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
-2l\cos\theta + OH & 2l\sin\theta & 0 \\
R_{Ex} & R_{Ey} & 0
\end{vmatrix}
= [(-2l\cos\theta + OH)R_{Ey} - (2l\sin\theta)R_{Ex}]\vec{k}$$

The left side of the angular momentum theorem is:
$$\frac{d\vec{\sigma}_A}{dt} = \frac{4}{3}ml^2\ddot{\theta}\vec{k}$$

So the angular momentum theorem gives:
$$\frac{4}{3}ml^2\ddot{\theta} = -mgl\cos\theta + [(-2l\cos\theta + OH)R_{Ey} - (2l\sin\theta)R_{Ex}]$$

We need another equation to find both components of $\vec{R}_E$. Let's use the momentum theorem:
$$m\vec{a}_G = \vec{F}_A + \vec{R}_E + m\vec{g}$$

The acceleration of $G$ is:
$$\vec{a}_G = \frac{d\vec{V}_G}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}[(v_A - l\dot{\theta}\sin\theta)\vec{i} + l\dot{\theta}\cos\theta\vec{j}]$$
$$= [-l\ddot{\theta}\sin\theta - l\dot{\theta}^2\cos\theta]\vec{i} + [l\ddot{\theta}\cos\theta - l\dot{\theta}^2\sin\theta]\vec{j}$$

So the momentum theorem gives:
$$m[-l\ddot{\theta}\sin\theta - l\dot{\theta}^2\cos\theta] = F_{Ax} + R_{Ex}$$
$$m[l\ddot{\theta}\cos\theta - l\dot{\theta}^2\sin\theta] = R_{Ey} - mg$$

We can solve these equations to find $R_{Ex}$ and $R_{Ey}$, but the expression will be quite complex in terms of $\theta$, $v_A$, $l$, and $OH$.

The final expression for $\vec{R}_E$ is:
$$\vec{R}_E = \left(-m[-l\ddot{\theta}\sin\theta - l\dot{\theta}^2\cos\theta] - F_{Ax}\right)\vec{i} + \left(m[l\ddot{\theta}\cos\theta - l\dot{\theta}^2\sin\theta] + mg\right)\vec{j}$$

Where $\dot{\theta}$ and $\ddot{\theta}$ are given in part 1.
\end{correctionbox}

% ----------- EXERCISE 5 -------------
\section{}

A material point $A$ of mass $m$ is fixed on the perimeter of a hoop $D$, of center $C$, radius $R$, and mass $M$. This system $S$ rolls without slipping on the axis $OX$ of a terrestrial reference frame $R(O, X, Y, Z)$ assumed to be Galilean. The motion takes place in the plane $XOY$. We denote by $\theta$ the position of point $A$ with respect to the hoop $D$, and $I$ the geometric contact point (fig 4).

\begin{enumerate}
    \item What is the number of degrees of freedom of the system?
    \item Calculate the velocities of points $I$, $C$, and $A$ with respect to $R$.
    \item Find the momentum of $S$.
    \item Determine the differential equation of motion, in $\theta$, of $S$ by applying the angular momentum theorem at point $I$.
    \item What is the nature of the motion of $S$?
\end{enumerate}

\begin{correctionbox}
\textbf{1. Number of degrees of freedom:}

The system consists of a hoop with a fixed point on its perimeter. The hoop rolls without slipping on a fixed line. This imposes one constraint. The system has one degree of freedom, which can be described by the angle $\theta$.

\textbf{2. Velocities of points $I$, $C$, and $A$:}

Let $\phi$ be the rotation angle of the hoop about its center $C$. Due to rolling without slipping, when the hoop rotates by an angle $\phi$, its center moves by $R\phi$ along OX.

So the position of center $C$ is:
$$\vec{OC} = R\phi\vec{i} + R\vec{j}$$

The velocity of $C$ is:
$$\vec{V}_C = R\dot{\phi}\vec{i}$$

Point $I$ is the contact point. Its position is:
$$\vec{OI} = R\phi\vec{i}$$

Since the hoop rolls without slipping, point $I$ has zero velocity relative to the ground:
$$\vec{V}_I = \vec{0}$$

Point $A$ is fixed on the hoop at angular position $\theta$ from the vertical. Its position relative to $C$ is:
$$\vec{CA} = R\sin\theta\vec{i} - R\cos\theta\vec{j}$$

So the position of $A$ is:
$$\vec{OA} = \vec{OC} + \vec{CA} = (R\phi + R\sin\theta)\vec{i} + (R - R\cos\theta)\vec{j}$$

The velocity of $A$ is:
$$\vec{V}_A = \frac{d\vec{OA}}{dt} = R\dot{\phi}\vec{i} + R\dot{\theta}\cos\theta\vec{i} + R\dot{\theta}\sin\theta\vec{j}$$
$$= R(\dot{\phi} + \dot{\theta}\cos\theta)\vec{i} + R\dot{\theta}\sin\theta\vec{j}$$

\textbf{3. Momentum of $S$:}

The system $S$ consists of the hoop $D$ (mass $M$) and the point mass $A$ (mass $m$).

The momentum is:
$$\vec{P}(S/R) = M\vec{V}_C + m\vec{V}_A$$
$$= MR\dot{\phi}\vec{i} + m[R(\dot{\phi} + \dot{\theta}\cos\theta)\vec{i} + R\dot{\theta}\sin\theta\vec{j}]$$
$$= R[(M + m)\dot{\phi} + m\dot{\theta}\cos\theta]\vec{i} + mR\dot{\theta}\sin\theta\vec{j}$$

\textbf{4. Differential equation of motion using angular momentum theorem at $I$:}

The angular momentum theorem at point $I$ states:
$$\frac{d\vec{\sigma}_I}{dt} = \vec{M}_I(\text{external forces})$$

The external forces are:
- Weight of hoop: $Mg$ acting at $C$
- Weight of point mass: $mg$ acting at $A$
- Normal reaction at $I$ (but its moment about $I$ is zero)
- Friction at $I$ (but its moment about $I$ is zero)

The angular momentum about $I$ has contributions from both the hoop and the point mass.

For the hoop, we use Koenig's theorem:
$$\vec{\sigma}_I(D/R) = \vec{\sigma}_C(D/R) + M\vec{IC} \times \vec{V}_C$$

We have $\vec{IC} = R\vec{j}$ and $\vec{V}_C = R\dot{\phi}\vec{i}$, so:
$$M\vec{IC} \times \vec{V}_C = M(R\vec{j}) \times (R\dot{\phi}\vec{i}) = MR^2\dot{\phi}(\vec{j} \times \vec{i}) = -MR^2\dot{\phi}\vec{k}$$

The hoop rotates about its center with angular velocity $\dot{\phi}$, so:
$$\vec{\sigma}_C(D/R) = I_C\dot{\phi}\vec{k}$$

For a hoop about its center:
$$I_C = MR^2$$

So:
$$\vec{\sigma}_C(D/R) = MR^2\dot{\phi}\vec{k}$$

Therefore:
$$\vec{\sigma}_I(D/R) = MR^2\dot{\phi}\vec{k} - MR^2\dot{\phi}\vec{k} = 0$$

This is interesting: the angular momentum of a rolling hoop about the contact point is zero.

For the point mass $A$:
$$\vec{\sigma}_I(A/R) = \vec{IA} \times m\vec{V}_A$$

We have $\vec{IA} = \vec{OA} - \vec{OI} = (R\phi + R\sin\theta - R\phi)\vec{i} + (R - R\cos\theta)\vec{j} = R\sin\theta\vec{i} + R(1 - \cos\theta)\vec{j}$

And $\vec{V}_A = R(\dot{\phi} + \dot{\theta}\cos\theta)\vec{i} + R\dot{\theta}\sin\theta\vec{j}$

So:
$$\vec{\sigma}_I(A/R) = m
\begin{vmatrix}
\vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\
R\sin\theta & R(1 - \cos\theta) & 0 \\
R(\dot{\phi} + \dot{\theta}\cos\theta) & R\dot{\theta}\sin\theta & 0
\end{vmatrix}
= mR^2[\sin\theta \cdot \dot{\theta}\sin\theta - (1 - \cos\theta)(\dot{\phi} + \dot{\theta}\cos\theta)]\vec{k}$$
$$= mR^2[\dot{\theta}\sin^2\theta - (1 - \cos\theta)\dot{\phi} - (1 - \cos\theta)\dot{\theta}\cos\theta]\vec{k}$$

The total angular momentum is:
$$\vec{\sigma}_I(S/R) = mR^2[\dot{\theta}\sin^2\theta - (1 - \cos\theta)\dot{\phi} - (1 - \cos\theta)\dot{\theta}\cos\theta]\vec{k}$$

Now, the moment of external forces about $I$:
$$\vec{M}_I = \vec{IC} \times M\vec{g} + \vec{IA} \times m\vec{g}$$
$$= (R\vec{j}) \times (-Mg\vec{j}) + [R\sin\theta\vec{i} + R(1 - \cos\theta)\vec{j}] \times (-mg\vec{j})$$
$$= 0 + (-mgR\sin\theta)(\vec{i} \times \vec{j}) = -mgR\sin\theta\vec{k}$$

The angular momentum theorem gives:
$$\frac{d}{dt}\left\{mR^2[\dot{\theta}\sin^2\theta - (1 - \cos\theta)\dot{\phi} - (1 - \cos\theta)\dot{\theta}\cos\theta]\right\} = -mgR\sin\theta$$

This is the differential equation of motion. It can be simplified using trigonometric identities.

\textbf{5. Nature of the motion:}

The motion is oscillatory about a stable equilibrium position. The system behaves like a pendulum, with the point mass $A$ oscillating about the vertical position.
